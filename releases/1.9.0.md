## What this release is about

1.8.0 taught FreeITSM to read a CardDAV address book. This one lets it write back. Correct a customer's phone number during the call and their contact card is updated as well, so nobody has to remember to fix the same thing twice in two applications.

It is off by default and stays off until you switch it on, because it is the difference between an integration that can only read your address book and one that can change it. When you do switch it on, it is deliberately careful: it edits only the detail that changed and leaves the rest of the card exactly as it was, and it refuses rather than overwrites if somebody has already changed the same thing at the other end.

There are also two new diagnostics for when it does not behave, and a log of every change FreeITSM has ever sent.

## New features

- **Send changes back to the address book.** On **System → Authentication → your address book → Contacts**, tick *Write changes made in FreeITSM back to the address book*. From then on, when an analyst corrects a contact's job title, department, office, phone or mobile, the contact card is updated too. Employee number and manager stay in FreeITSM, because a contact card has nowhere to keep them - which also means those two are now yours to fill in freely on an imported contact.

- **Nothing else on the card is touched.** FreeITSM changes the single detail that changed and leaves everything else exactly as it found it: photo, notes, birthday, the full street address, group membership, and anything your other systems have added. It does not rebuild the card from what it happens to know, which is the usual way an integration quietly strips a contact of everything it does not understand.

- **It refuses rather than overwrites.** Before writing, FreeITSM re-reads the card and compares it with what it last imported. If somebody has changed that same detail in the address book in the meantime, the change is refused and you are told what their version says. If they changed something else, that is left alone and your change goes through.

- **Check permission before you commit to it.** A new button asks the server what your account is actually allowed to do, without writing anything. Shared and subscribed address books are very often read-only however correct your password is, and it is better to know that here than to find out halfway through somebody's edit.

- **A log of every change sent.** The **History** tab now has a *Changes sent back* section: who, when, which details, and what the server itself said, kept word for word. When an address book refuses something the reason is in its own reply, and that is the thing to forward to whoever runs the server.

- **Two new tools under System → Debug Tools.** **CardDAV health** walks the connection one step at a time - reach the server, sign in, read the book, and may this account write to it - because those fail for completely different reasons and one verdict leaves you four things to check. **CardDAV drift** compares every imported person with their contact card, detail by detail, and tells you which details have drifted apart. Neither writes anything.

## Improvements

- **You are always told what happened to the address book.** A save that reached the card says so; one that did not says what stopped it, and your change is still saved in FreeITSM either way. An address book that is switched off or unreachable costs a couple of seconds rather than leaving the form hanging.

- **In-app help for all of it**, including eight troubleshooting entries written for what actually goes wrong: the details are greyed out, it says saved but the card never changes, everything is being refused, the account may not write. Searching System for "refused", "drift" or "not writing" now finds them.

## Fixes

- **An imported contact's employee number and manager could not be filled in.** They were greyed out and refused on save, on behalf of an import that was never going to supply them - a contact card has nowhere to keep either. They are editable now. People imported from a directory are unaffected; a directory really does own all seven details.

- **A job title containing a comma or a semicolon imported with a stray backslash in it.** "Head, Service Desk" arrived as "Head\, Service Desk". Phone numbers with an extension could be affected the same way. Existing contacts are corrected on the next import.

- **The Debug Tools page showed a PHP warning inside one of its cards.** The Azure OpenAI tile displayed an error message where its badge should have been. Present since that tool was added.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

Nothing else is required. Sending changes back is off until you switch it on, and if you never do, FreeITSM continues to read your address book and nothing else - exactly as it did in 1.8.0.

If you do switch it on, run **CardDAV drift** first. Contacts that have already drifted apart are exactly the ones whose edits will be refused, and it is easier to see that in a report than to discover it one puzzled analyst at a time.

## Rollback

Safe - this release only adds to the database, so you can go back to 1.8.0.
