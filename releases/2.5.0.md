## What this release is about

Two things most people will notice. The **self-service portal** can now be made to
look like yours rather than like the admin side — its own logo, its own colours,
an optional background — and the people who use it can do rather more for
themselves: see the equipment assigned to them, close their own ticket, and read
the knowledge base the way they prefer.

The other is **equipment belonging to the IT team**. Until now an asset could only
be assigned to somebody you support, which meant the spare laptop, the loan phone
and the test handset had nowhere to live. Assets can now be assigned to analysts
too, and carry a **lease end date** alongside the warranty date, with alerts
before either one arrives.

Also here: **checklists can now block a ticket closing**, contributed by somebody
outside the project; a **second assignment email written for the analyst** rather
than the customer, asked for by a user of FreeITSM; and tasks can be **moved
between companies**.

Everything is translated. All thirteen complete languages are at 100% for this
release, not just for the last one.

## New features

- **Brand the self-service portal separately from the app.** A new **System →
  Self-service portal** screen gives the portal its own logo, its own header and
  accent colours, and an optional subtle background pattern — dots, a grid,
  diagonals or topography. A live preview sits beside the settings. Doing nothing
  leaves the portal exactly as it was.

- **People can close their own ticket.** For the commonest wasted ticket on any
  desk: *it started working*, *I sorted it myself*, *I raised this twice*. They
  can add a reason, which is recorded on the ticket. The history says plainly that
  the requester closed it rather than crediting whichever analyst was assigned.
  Off by default.

- **People can see the equipment assigned to them.** A **My equipment** page, so
  somebody can read a serial number off the screen instead of raising a ticket to
  ask what it is. Off by default, because not every organisation wants it.

- **Four ways to read the portal knowledge base**, remembered per person —
  **Cards**, **List**, **Tree** and **Table**, with the same four names the
  analyst side uses, so nobody has to learn two vocabularies for one idea.

- **Assign an asset to an analyst, not just to a user.** Equipment held by the
  desk itself had nowhere to live: assignment only ever offered the people you
  support, and on most installs the people running the desk have no user record at
  all. The assign dialog now has a **User / Analyst** switch, and the holder shown
  on the asset carries a small **Analyst** tag so the two are never confused.

- **Lease end dates, and alerts before they arrive.** An asset carries a **Lease
  ends** date beside its warranty date. A date that has passed, or is within 30
  days, is flagged on the asset itself. **Assets → Settings → Warranty** gains a
  matching block for leases with its own warning window and its own choice of
  where they appear — a figure on the **Watchtower** dashboard, entries in the
  **Calendar** under their own Lease category, both, or nowhere. The default
  window is 60 days rather than the 30 used for warranties, because returning or
  renewing leased equipment takes arranging.

- **Move a task to another company.** A task raised against the wrong client used
  to mean deleting it and typing it again, losing its comments, its time entries
  and its history along with the mistake. Right-click a task and **Move to
  company**, from the board, the table or the timeline. Subtasks move with it.
  Invisible on a single-company install.

- **Checklists can stop a ticket being closed.** Contributed by Santhosh
  Srinivasan, from [discussion #138](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/discussions/138).
  Each checklist template is **Standard** — warns and records who overrode it — or
  **Critical**, which refuses the close outright. **Tickets → Settings →
  Checklists** can override that for the whole company, and decides what happens
  to a ticket with no checklist attached at all.

- **A second assignment email, this one written for the analyst.** Asked for in
  [#148](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/148) by An Duong. The
  assignment email has always gone to the person who raised the ticket, which is
  what they need to hear. There is now a separate **Assigned to an analyst** event
  with its own wording and a link into the inbox rather than the customer's portal
  view. Configure one, both or neither. It is deliberately **not** sent when you
  assign a ticket to yourself. The templates screen now says **who each template
  is sent to** — requester or assigned analyst — beside which senders it covers.

## Improvements

- **The training screen in the portal runs full width**, and a completed course
  now says *when* it was completed rather than only that it was.

- **The Watchtower Assets card counts leases as well as warranties**, and no
  longer claims all is well while a lease alert sits above it.

- **Thirteen languages are complete for this release**, not merely for the last
  one — Danish, German, Spanish, French, Gujarati, Hindi, Malayalam, Malay,
  Norwegian Bokmål, Norwegian Nynorsk, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese and
  Ukrainian. Every string added in this release exists in all thirteen.

- **FreeITSM is rated 9.4/10 by iTechGuides**, and the badge now sits in the
  footer of the website.

## Fixes

- **Light or dark in the portal would not stick** for anybody who was also signed
  in as an analyst — the analyst's theme quietly won. Both halves of the feature
  worked, which is why it survived so long: the people most likely to notice were
  the ones least likely to be believed about it.

- **An asset held by an analyst would have appeared to be held by nobody**, with
  the count beside it one short. Six places that display a holder were corrected,
  including the record preview, where the count and the name came from different
  queries and would have contradicted each other on screen.

- **An asset whose recorded holder no longer exists** is no longer shown as held
  by somebody with no name.

- **The Checklists screens showed in English in every language** — and no coverage
  report could see it, because the screen had never been wired up for translation
  at all, so the strings were missing from the English source too.

- **A dashboard widget described itself as "Tickets created per dai."**

- **The satisfaction survey greeted two languages wrongly** when the customer's
  name was not known.

- **A workflow closing a ticket no longer credits the audit note to somebody who
  did not do it.**

- **Moving a task now honours a company scope narrower than the analyst's own
  access.** For a signed-in person the two are always the same, so this would have
  looked correct indefinitely; an API key issued for one company could have moved
  work into a company it was deliberately not given.

- **Database Verification reported that two new indexes were missing from the
  list it restores them from**, and it was right. Nothing was lost or at risk;
  it means an install that had lost either index could not have got it back.
  Both are created by Database Verification in this release.

- **Grey body text on the purple and amber page headings** on the website was
  almost unreadable, and is now white.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

Nothing else to do by hand. Every new feature in this release is off, or
unchanged in behaviour, until you switch it on.

## Rollback

Safe — this release only adds to the database, so you can go back to 2.4.0 with
the code alone.
